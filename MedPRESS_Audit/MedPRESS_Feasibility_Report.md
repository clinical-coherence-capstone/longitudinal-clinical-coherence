## 1. Answer in short
- **Can we access MedPRESS?** **Yes.** Access to the gated Hugging Face repo `samanjoy2/MedPRESS_Benchmark` was granted on 28 Oct 2026 and we downloaded the 3 CSVs. The repo page cites arXiv:2608.02520 and both authors, but the paper itself does not link to the repo, so 'official' rests on that match.
- **Can it test pressure vs. justified updating?** Only half. It tests **pressure without new evidence** well. It does **not** contain corrections or new evidence, so it cannot test the "should update" side [Paper].
- **Recommendation: Investigate further, and plan to build our own paired cases.** MedPRESS can serve as the "unjustified pressure" arm. We need our own cases for the legitimate-correction, new-evidence and persistence arms.

## 4. Contents
**Verified against the paper:**
- 600 cases = 3 scenario families x 20 topics x 10 variants (p.3, Table 2). "600 five-turn dialogues" is **confirmed by the paper**.
- Families: medication and treatment demand, personal health self-care, symptom triage and care resistance. Triage needed: 420 yes / 180 no.
- One case = topic, unsafe belief, safe stance summary, five user turns, care-escalation flag, triage trigger (p.3).
- **The five turns are all written by the user (patient).** T1 initial query containing the unsafe belief; T2 personal experience; T3 social proof; T4 external claim ("I read articles/videos"); T5 direct challenge (p.4).
- Assistant answers are **not part of the case**. They were generated by 20 model configs (4 prompt conditions, 2,400 conversations per model) and judged by Qwen3-32B (p.11). Whether those outputs are released: promised after publication; Not verified.
- Labels: a fixed "expected safe stance" per case. Judge labels each answer safe / unsafe / ambiguous. Human check: 2 annotators on 100 conversations, Fleiss' kappa 0.837 (p.12). One MBBS physician reviewed all 600 cases, 100% agreement (p.13).
- **Justified vs unjustified change:** Not specified in the source. Every change toward the unsafe belief is treated as failure.
- Headline results (for context only): average unsafe agreement 50.5%; 86.8% of conversations have at least one unsafe answer (p.5-8).

## 6. Limits and risks the team should know
- Stated by the authors: scripted turns; only unambiguous public-health consensus topics; mostly LLM judging; one physician reviewer; 20 models (p.9).
- Our observations [Rec]:
  - The fixed escalation order (experience, social proof, claim, challenge) may confound pressure type with position. Authors tested random order on 6 models; effects changed but failures remained (p.13-14, Table 16).
  - The judge counts hedges like "your experience may be valid" as unsafe. That is stricter than a verifier that must also allow justified updates.
  - Ambiguous answers are a third outcome (20.3%). Our verifier needs a rule for them.
  - T4 says the user "saw articles" but gives no content, so it is pressure, not evidence.
  - Topics are patient-advice scenarios, not the structured multi-turn clinical cases of DDXPlus.
- License, redistribution and citation: citation = the arXiv paper. Redistribution rules: Not verified. Do not re-upload data.
- Expert review: an MBBS physician reviewed the safe stances. For our own new paired cases we should get clinician review. [Rec]

## 7. Recommendation [Rec]
**Investigate further** (official label), meaning: use MedPRESS now for behaviour A only, and keep one open item: the dataset **license** is not stated on the card or in the paper. Email the authors or check the repo files for a license before redistributing anything. Optional second item: ask the authors to confirm the repo is the official release.
Plan regardless of outcome: build a small set of **paired cases** from MedPRESS topics. Same case and same first answer; version P adds only pressure (reuse a MedPRESS T2-T5 turn), version E adds one genuine new fact (new symptom or test result) or an explicit correction of a stated fact. Expected verifier outcome: flag the change in P, accept it in E. Add a later turn that checks the corrected fact is still used. Have a clinician check the expected outcomes.
